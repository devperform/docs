DevPerform connects to your code host — **GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket** — and surfaces the metrics that matter for engineering teams: how long PRs sit before review, how often work ships, and where the bottlenecks are.

## What DevPerform measures

DevPerform analyzes your pull/merge request history to compute:

- **Cycle time** — how long from PR open to merge
- **Time to first review** — how long PRs wait before someone looks at them
- **Merge rate** — what fraction of opened PRs actually ship
- **Review throughput** — how many PRs each engineer reviews per period
- **PR size distribution** — the breakdown of XS/S/M/L/XL PRs per engineer
- **DORA** — deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate, time to restore

These metrics update as you sync from your host — no agents or CI plugins required. Everything works the same whether your team is on GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket.

## What DevPerform does not do

DevPerform does not track commits, lines of code as a productivity proxy, or individual keystroke activity. The goal is to help teams spot process problems, not to score engineers.

## How it works

1. Sign in with **GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket** — whichever your team uses
2. An admin connects the org / group / workspace and adds the repos to track
3. Engineers are added to a roster (or imported in one click) with their handles
4. DevPerform syncs PR/MR data from your host and computes metrics from it
5. The dashboard shows team and per-engineer breakdowns for any date range
